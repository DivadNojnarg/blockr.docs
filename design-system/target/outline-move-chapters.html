Moving blocks vs moving chapters

One rule: the handle you grab decides the scope. Grab a chip and you move a block (and it joins whatever chapter surrounds where it lands). Grab a chapter heading and the whole chapter travels, membership intact, landing only at chapter boundaries.

The board below has two chapters fed by the same source — the case you described, where either order is legitimate:

data ─▶ prep(head) ─┬─▶ Charts (plot A, plot B) └─▶ Tables (table)
Drag a chip = block · drag a heading = whole chapter
Try both: drag "Bar chart" somewhere, then drag the "Charts" heading below "Tables". Only legal landings are offered.

What the mock shows

  • Chapter drag lands only at boundaries. While dragging a heading, the lines inside chapters are not offered — a chapter cannot land inside another chapter, because the model has no sub-chapters. Dragging a chip offers every legal gap instead. The two modes are visibly different without a word of explanation.
  • Legality is the DAG, one level up. A chapter may move past another only if no block in it depends on a block it would jump. "Charts" and "Tables" are parallel, so both orders are offered; neither may move above "Data prep", which feeds them — that boundary is simply never highlighted.
  • Collapse, then drag. Collapse a chapter (click its title) and it becomes one compact row — the classic outliner move: fold, drag, unfold. It also stops you dragging a 300-pixel-tall object around.
  • Blocks still follow Rule 1: a chip that lands inside another chapter joins it; the drop line names the chapter before you release.
Deliberately absent: dropping a chapter onto another to merge them. "Merge up" already does that, and overloading the drop would bring back the two-meanings-for-one-target problem. Split chapters ("(continued)") are also not draggable — that shape is forced by dependencies, so moving one run of it is ambiguous.
