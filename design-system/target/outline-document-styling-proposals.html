Outline document styling — title, lead, collapse

Third round on the outline's document column (blockr.outline). Verdict on the current state: the tinted intro boxes read as callouts, not as a chapter's lead prose; the chapter titles are too small to carry a document; the chevron floats. Principle for this round: color's job is grouping, typography's job is hierarchy — the stack color should hold a chapter together without ever painting the prose. Three proposals, each showing title + lead + a section + an excluded stub + a collapsed chapter. Click any chapter title to collapse it.

Baseline — what ships today baseline

Small bar-prefixed title, stack-tinted lead box, chevron hanging in the gutter gap. The lead box is the main offender: a colored panel says "callout / warning", while a chapter lead is just prose.

A — editorial: kicker + whitespace, color only as a label

Pure document typography. The stack asserts itself as a tiny uppercase kicker in the stack color above the title (the only colored ink), the title is a real heading, the lead is a classic standfirst: slightly larger, muted, no box. Grouping comes from whitespace rhythm — generous above a chapter, tight inside it. Weakness: on a long chapter the color cue is gone once the kicker scrolls away, so color "keeps stuff together" only weakly.

B — thread: one colored line brackets the whole chapter recommended

The stack color becomes a single thin line running down the chapter's left edge — title, lead and every section hang off the same thread (the pattern of a Slack thread or a diff gutter). This is "color keeps stuff together" in its most literal form: the line's extent IS the grouping, and it stays visible however long the chapter gets. Prose is never tinted. The lead is a plain standfirst as in A. Collapsing shrinks the thread to a stub next to the title — the collapse state is visible in the structure itself, not just in a rotated chevron.

C — header band: title underlined in the stack color, lead inside the header

The chapter is introduced by a header unit: title with a short thick underline in the stack color (a "tab" of ink, not a box), the lead directly beneath, and a hairline rule closing the header before the sections start. Reads like a paper's section heading with abstract. Color is a compact, strong signal at the top; like A it does not extend down the chapter, and the hairline adds horizontal structure the outline otherwise avoids.

Shared decisions (all variants)

  • Title is a real heading: 1.15rem semibold ink, chevron at its left in the heading's own row (no gutter-hanging), whole title row clicks to collapse.
  • Lead is prose, not a panel: slightly larger than body, muted grey, no background, no border. It is the document's standfirst.
  • Collapsed state: chevron rotates, body hides, a muted "N blocks" count appears inline after the title — text, not a pill.
  • The gutter rail keeps its spine as today: chips stay grouped by the same color on the left, so the color story is consistent across both columns.
  • Splits: "(continued)" runs repeat the chapter treatment without the lead.

Recommendation: B. It is the only variant where the color actually performs the grouping job over the chapter's full length — the thread bracket makes "what belongs to this chapter" visible at any scroll position, mirrors the gutter spine on the left, and the collapse animation (the line shrinking to a stub) explains itself. A's kicker ("Chapter 1") can be grafted onto B later if numbering is wanted in the rendered report too.
