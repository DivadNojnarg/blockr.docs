Moving between chapters vs reordering

Two same-style links in the block row — + New block and + New chapter — settle creation. What remains is the drag: how does moving a block to another chapter differ from simply reordering it? The proposal below is that it doesn't have to.

The two links (both panes)

Identical styling, same row, same weight — one adds a block after this one, the other starts a chapter at this one. Hover any block row in the mocks to see them.

Rule 1 — position implies membership recommended

A chapter is a contiguous run of the document. So wherever a block lands, it belongs to the chapter that surrounds that place: drop it inside Analysis's span and it is in Analysis; drop it between two chapters and it joins the one above. One gesture, no modes, nothing to distinguish — and the drop line tells you the consequence in advance by taking the target chapter's colour and naming it.

Rule 1 — drag a chip; the line shows which chapter it will join
Drag "Iris data" down into Analysis — it becomes an Analysis block.

Rule 2 — membership is explicit the alternative

Dragging into a gap only reorders and the block keeps its chapter; changing chapter needs a separate act (dropping on the heading). Honest about intent, but it produces the outcome nobody asks for: a block sitting visually inside Analysis while still belonging to Data prep, which the document must then render as a split — "Data prep (continued)". Watch the mock do exactly that.

Rule 2 — drag "Iris data" into Analysis: it stays Data prep and splits the chapter

What each rule costs

  • Rule 1 cannot express "put this block here but keep it in its old chapter" — deliberate splits become impossible. In practice splits are something the dependency graph forces on you, not something you want, so this reads as a feature.
  • Rule 1 needs a boundary convention: a drop exactly between two chapters joins the one above (shown by the line's colour before you release).
  • Rule 2 keeps drag-onto-heading as a second, redundant way to change chapter, and every accidental cross-chapter drop creates a "(continued)" run the author has to notice and undo.
  • Both keep "ungroup" for dissolving a chapter, and under Rule 1 the missing take out of chapter gesture disappears: drop the block at a boundary or into an unchaptered stretch and it is out.
Proposal: two identical links for creation (+ New block, + New chapter), and Rule 1 for the drag — position implies membership, the drop line names the chapter you are about to join, and "drag onto the heading" stays as a convenience that means the same thing. Nothing then has two meanings, and there is no mode to explain.
