Outline reorder — the affordance for a rare action

Fourth round. Findings against the shipped hover-grip: (1) the grip participates in the chip's flex row, so rows with a grip have their switch at a different x than pinned rows — content shifts; (2) a six-dot glyph that appears on hover explains nothing — "how could I move these blocks?". Constraints: reordering is rare (only DAG-parallel neighbours qualify at all), so the affordance must cost nothing when unused, must not disturb alignment, and must explain itself when it matters. Mock: "Analysis subset" is pinned; "Sepal scatter" and "QC glance" are the swappable pair.

Baseline shipped

Hover-revealed six-dot grip right of the switch, rendered only for movable blocks — misaligns the switch column and communicates nothing.

A — explicit reorder mode

A small "Reorder" toggle in the outline toolbar. Normal mode shows zero reorder chrome — perfect alignment, nothing to explain. Entering the mode dims everything immutable and turns the movable chips into obvious drag targets (dashed outline, grip, grab cursor). A mode is heavy machinery, but this is the classic treatment for rare-but-real rearranging (photo apps, playlist edit mode), and it doubles as the place where future bulk operations (restack, renumber) could live.

B — overlay grip (minimal repair)

Keep the shipped behaviour but position the grip absolutely, floating in the column gap outside the chip — it occupies no flex space, so every switch aligns whether a block is movable or not. Cheapest fix; still hover-only and still mute about what dragging means. Honest framing: this repairs the looks complaint but not the discoverability one.

D — boundary swap recommended

Drop dragging entirely. Wherever two adjacent sections are genuinely order-free, a small "⇅ swap" pill sits on the boundary between them (revealed while the outline is hovered). One click swaps the pair. The affordance appears exactly where a choice exists and nowhere else, needs no explanation, and no row carries any chrome — alignment is untouched by construction. Any permutation of a longer parallel run is reachable by adjacent swaps. This also matches the reality that reordering decisions are local: "should the QC glance come before or after the plot?"

C — row menu (for completeness)

A tiny ⋮ on chip hover opening "Move up / Move down" entries, enabled only where valid. Familiar and explicit, but it adds a menu layer for two actions the boundary pill expresses in one click, and the hover-⋮ has the same discoverability ceiling as the grip. Not mocked; listed for the record.

Recommendation: D. It deletes the drag machinery instead of decorating it: a labelled one-click pill exactly at each order-free boundary. Nothing to learn, nothing rendered anywhere else, alignment untouched. If boards with long parallel runs ever make pairwise swapping tedious, A's explicit mode is the escalation path — the two compose (D always on, A added later) without conflict.
